# DPDP breach intimation: 72 hours, 6 hours and "without delay"

A lost laptop with a customer list, a WhatsApp group shared with the wrong person, a hacked website form or a misconfigured Google Sheet can all be a "personal data breach". From **13 May 2027**, Rule 7 of the DPDP Rules, 2025 tells a business exactly whom to inform and what to say ([Rule 1(4), Rule 7](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)). Separately, the CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 already require reporting certain cyber incidents within 6 hours.

This page sets out the three clocks in plain language.

## What counts as a personal data breach

Section 2(u) of the DPDP Act, 2023 defines it as "any unauthorised processing of personal data or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction or loss of access to personal data, that compromises the confidentiality, integrity or availability of personal data".

There is **no materiality threshold** in the definition. A small incident affecting a few customers is still a breach. Keep a breach register and log every incident, even if you later decide it was minor.

## Clock 1: tell each affected person "without delay" (Rule 7(1))

On becoming aware of a breach, the business must, to the best of its knowledge, inform **each affected Data Principal** "in a concise, clear and plain manner and without delay", through her user account or any mode of communication she has registered with you (for example, her email or mobile number). The message must include:

| Rule 7(1) | What to say |
|---|---|
| (a) | A description of the breach: its nature, extent and the timing of its occurrence |
| (b) | The consequences relevant to her that are likely to arise |
| (c) | The measures you have taken and are taking to mitigate risk, if any |
| (d) | The safety measures she may take to protect her interests (for example, change a password, watch for fraud calls) |
| (e) | Business contact information of a person who can respond to her queries on your behalf |

"Without delay" has no number of hours. Our interpretation: send it as soon as you know enough to say something accurate and useful, and update later if needed.

## Clock 2: tell the Data Protection Board (Rule 7(2))

The Board must be informed in two stages:

**(a) Without delay:** a description of the breach, including its nature, extent, timing and **location** of occurrence, and the likely impact.

**(b) Within seventy-two hours** of becoming aware (or a longer period the Board allows on a written request):

1. updated and detailed information on that description;
2. the broad facts about the events, circumstances and reasons leading to the breach;
3. measures implemented or proposed to mitigate risk;
4. any findings about the person who caused the breach;
5. remedial measures taken to prevent recurrence; and
6. a report on the intimations given to affected Data Principals.

**Filing channel: not yet confirmed.** The PIB explainer of 17 Nov 2025 describes a digital-first Board with an online portal. News reports in November 2026 said the Board still had no appointed members ([ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/govt-opens-registration-for-consent-managers-under-dpdp-act-rs-2-crore-net-worth-required20261114145151/), [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-companies-consent-managers-dpdp-act/)). Check the Board's official website for the breach-intimation channel before 13 May 2027.

## Clock 3: CERT-In within 6 hours (already in force)

The CERT-In Directions issued under s.70B of the IT Act on 28 April 2022 ([PDF](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf)) apply to service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates and government organisations:

- **Report within 6 hours** of noticing, or being told about, any incident type in **Annexure I**. The list includes unauthorised access of IT systems or data, website defacement or intrusion, malicious code and ransomware, phishing and identity theft, attacks on servers and applications such as e-commerce, **data breach** and **data leak**.
- Report by email to **incident@cert-in.org.in**, phone 1800-11-4949 or fax 1800-11-6969 (direction (ii)).
- **Keep logs** of all ICT systems securely for a rolling **180 days**, maintained **within the Indian jurisdiction** (direction (iv)).
- **Synchronise clocks** with NIC or NPL NTP servers, or sources traceable to them (direction (i)).
- Designate a Point of Contact for CERT-In (direction (iii)).

CERT-In reporting is a separate legal duty. Reporting to CERT-In does not replace intimation to the Board or to affected people, and the reverse is also true.

## A simple breach timeline

| Time from noticing | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 0 h | Log the incident: who noticed, when, what data, how many people | Good practice; s.2(u) |
| Within 1 h | Contain: reset access, take the form offline, call your web developer | Good practice |
| Within 6 h | Report to CERT-In if it is an Annexure I type | CERT-In Directions (ii) |
| As soon as possible | Initial description to the Board | Rule 7(2)(a) |
| As soon as possible | Message to each affected person with items (a)–(e) | Rule 7(1) |
| Within 72 h of becoming aware | Detailed report to the Board, items (i)–(vi), or written request for more time | Rule 7(2)(b) |
| After | Fix the cause, update your security measures, close the register entry | Rule 6(1) |

## If you use a processor

If your host, CRM, SMS gateway or WhatsApp API provider has the breach, you remain responsible as the Data Fiduciary (s.8(1)). Your contract should require them to tell you quickly, with the facts you need for Rule 7 (see Rule 6(1)(f)).

## Penalties, stated plainly

Under the Schedule to the Act, failing to give the Board or affected Data Principals intimation of a breach (s.8(6)) can attract a penalty that "may extend to two hundred crore rupees". Failing to take reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5)) may extend to two hundred and fifty crore rupees. These are maximums. The Board first holds an inquiry (s.28) and imposes a penalty only if it finds the breach significant (s.33(1)). In setting the amount, it must consider factors such as the nature, gravity and duration of the breach and whether, and how quickly, the business acted to mitigate it (s.33(2)). Prompt, honest intimation is the best protection.

## Breach readiness checklist

- [ ] A named person who decides "is this a breach?" and who reports
- [ ] A breach register with noticed time, aware time, location, data types and number of people
- [ ] Customer contact list you can export quickly (email, mobile)
- [ ] Draft Rule 7(1) message in English and Hindi, ready to fill
- [ ] Draft Rule 7(2) Board report outline with items (a) and (b)(i)–(vi)
- [ ] CERT-In email template and point of contact
- [ ] Logs kept for at least one year (Rule 6(1)(e), Rule 8(3)) and 180 days in India (CERT-In)
- [ ] Processor contracts with a breach-notice clause

## FAQ

**Do I have to report a small breach affecting only a few people?**
Section 2(u) has no size threshold, and Rule 7 has none either. Every personal data breach needs intimation to the Board and affected people.

**When does the 72-hour clock start?**
From when you "become aware" of the breach (Rule 7(2)(b)). The CERT-In 6-hour clock starts from "noticing" the incident or being told about it.

**What if I don't have all the facts in 72 hours?**
Rule 7(2)(b) allows a longer period if the Board permits it on a written request. Send what you know and ask for more time in writing.

**Can I tell customers on WhatsApp?**
Rule 7(1) allows her user account or "any mode of communication registered by her" with you. If she gave you her WhatsApp number for communication, that may qualify. Keep proof of sending.
