# DPDP privacy notice format in Hindi

Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, every request for consent must be "accompanied or preceded by a notice" (s.5(1)). Rule 3 of the DPDP Rules, 2025 says what that notice must contain. And s.5(3) says the person must have the option to read it in English or in any language listed in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution, which includes Hindi.

This page explains the required contents, the language question, and gives a short sample structure in English and Hindi that you can adapt with your lawyer. These duties apply from **13 May 2027** (Rule 1(4)).

## What Rule 3 requires

Rule 3 has three parts.

**Rule 3(a): standalone.** The notice must be "presented and be understandable independently of any other information". A line that says "see our terms" does not meet this. The notice should make sense on its own.

**Rule 3(b): a fair account in clear and plain language**, including at the minimum:

- (i) "an itemised description of such personal data". List the actual items: name, mobile number, email, address, date of birth, prescription details. Not "your information".
- (ii) "the specified purpose or purposes of, and specific description of the goods or services to be provided or uses to be enabled by, such processing". Say what you will do: "send appointment reminders on WhatsApp", not "improve services".

**Rule 3(c): the communication link and other means** by which the person can:

- (i) withdraw consent, "with the ease of doing so being comparable to that with which such consent was given";
- (ii) exercise her rights under the Act;
- (iii) make a complaint to the Data Protection Board of India.

The Act adds:

- The contact of a Data Protection Officer (if any) or a person authorised to respond, in every consent request (s.6(3)). Rule 9 also asks you to publish this contact prominently.
- The grievance route. A person must use your grievance process before going to the Board (s.13(3)), so explain it.

## The language question

Section 5(3) gives the person "the option to access the contents of the notice … in English or any language specified in the Eighth Schedule". Section 6(3) says the same for consent requests.

Lawyers and vendors read this differently. Some say you must offer all 22 scheduled languages. Others say you must offer the languages your customers actually use. As of November 2026 the Board had issued no guidance.

A practical approach many small businesses take (not legal advice): start with English and Hindi, add the main language of your state or customers, and record which language each person was shown. If you serve mostly Marathi, Tamil or Bengali speakers, start with that language.

**Translation tip:** use plain, everyday Hindi. Avoid heavy Sanskritised words that customers do not use. Keep legal terms like "Data Protection Board of India" in brackets after the Hindi so there is no doubt what is meant. Get a native speaker to read it before you publish.

## Sample notice structure (English)

This is a skeleton, not a finished notice. Replace the brackets and have a lawyer review it.

```
Privacy notice - [Business name]
Version [1.0], effective [date]

Who we are
[Business name], [address]. Website: [link].

What data we collect and why
1. Appointment booking
   Data: name, mobile number, preferred date and time.
   Purpose: to book your appointment and send reminders by SMS or WhatsApp.
2. Marketing messages (only if you say yes)
   Data: name, mobile number.
   Purpose: to send you offers and health tips.

Your choices
You can withdraw your consent at any time at [link] or by replying STOP.
It is as easy as giving consent.

Your rights
You can ask for a summary of your data, correct or update it, ask us to erase it,
nominate someone to act for you, or raise a grievance. Use [link] and share your
[registered mobile number]. We reply within [30] days.

Questions
[Name or role], [email], [phone].

Complaints
If you are not satisfied with our reply, you can complain to the
Data Protection Board of India at [Board portal link].
```

## Common mistakes

- **One vague purpose for everything.** "To provide and improve our services" is not specific. Split purposes and itemise data for each (Rule 3(b)).
- **Pre-ticked boxes or "by continuing you agree".** Consent needs a "clear affirmative action" (s.6(1)).
- **Withdrawal only by post or email.** If consent took one tap, withdrawal should be about as easy (s.6(4)).
- **Hindi text that is a machine translation nobody checked.** It can change the meaning. Have a native speaker review it.
- **No Board complaint line.** It is required by s.5(1)(iii) and Rule 3(c)(iii). The Board's official complaint portal was not yet live when we last checked; the PIB explainer says complaints will be filed online through a portal and app. Keep a placeholder and update the link when it is published.
- **Notice for old customers forgotten.** Data collected before the Act needs a notice too, "as soon as it is reasonably practicable" (s.5(2)).

## FAQ

**Do I need a separate notice for each form?**
You need a notice that covers the data and purposes of that form. Many businesses keep one full notice page and show a short notice next to each form with a link to the full page. Check with your lawyer that the short version is still understandable on its own.

**Is Hindi mandatory?**
The Act requires the option of English or an Eighth Schedule language. Hindi is one of them. Whether you must offer more languages is not settled.

**Can my privacy policy and DPDP notice be the same page?**
They can share a page, but the notice parts listed in Rule 3 must be clear and standalone. A long legal policy that buries them is risky.

**Do I need to keep old versions of my notice?**
The law does not prescribe a format, but you must be able to prove notice was given (s.6(10)). Keeping each version with its date is the simplest way.

**What if I collect data only on WhatsApp?**
The same rules apply. Send the notice (or a link to it) before or with the consent request in the chat.
