# Consent Manager vs consent tool: what is the difference under DPDP?

Since November 2026, Indian companies can apply to the Data Protection Board to register as a **Consent Manager**. At the same time, many software products sell "consent management" for websites. The two sound alike but are very different things in law. Mixing them up can confuse your customers and, for a software vendor, can create real legal risk.

## What the Act says a Consent Manager is

Section 2(g) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 defines a Consent Manager as:

> "a person registered with the Board, who acts as a single point of contact to enable a Data Principal to give, manage, review and withdraw her consent through an accessible, transparent and interoperable platform".

Section 6 adds:

- a Data Principal "may give, manage, review or withdraw her consent to the Data Fiduciary through a Consent Manager" (s.6(7));
- the Consent Manager is **accountable to the Data Principal** and acts on her behalf (s.6(8));
- every Consent Manager **must be registered with the Board** (s.6(9)).

So a Consent Manager works for the **individual**, across **many businesses**, from one platform. The PIB explainer of 17 Nov 2025 puts it the same way: "a single, transparent and interoperable platform through which a Data Principal may give, manage, review or withdraw consent" ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)).

## Registration conditions (Rule 4 and First Schedule)

Rule 4 of the DPDP Rules, 2025 came into force one year after publication, in November 2026 (Rule 1(3)). The First Schedule, Part A, sets the conditions for registration ([Rules PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)), including:

| Part A item | Condition |
|---|---|
| 1 | The applicant is a **company incorporated in India** |
| 2–3 | Sufficient technical, operational and financial capacity; sound finances and management |
| 4 | **Net worth of not less than two crore rupees** |
| 6 | Directors and senior management with a reputation for fairness and integrity |
| 7 | Memorandum and articles that lock in conflict-of-interest obligations |
| 9 | **Independent certification** that the interoperable platform meets the Board's data protection standards |

Part B lists ongoing obligations, for example:

- records of consents given, denied or withdrawn, and of the notices shown, kept for **at least seven years** (items 3 and 4);
- sharing designed so that the Consent Manager cannot read the data it routes (item 2);
- no sub-contracting of its obligations (item 6);
- acting in a **fiduciary capacity** towards the Data Principal (item 8);
- avoiding conflicts of interest with Data Fiduciaries, including their promoters and key managerial personnel (items 9 and 10);
- publishing details of its promoters, directors and major shareholders (item 11).

News reports in November 2026 said MeitY opened the registration portal around 13–14 Nov 2026 ([ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/govt-opens-registration-for-consent-managers-under-dpdp-act-rs-2-crore-net-worth-required20261114145151/), [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-companies-consent-managers-dpdp-act/)). Check the Board's official website for the current process.

## What a consent tool is

A consent tool, such as a website consent banner, a preference centre or a consent-records database, is software that **one business** uses for **its own** website, app or forms. With it, the business:

- shows its own notice (s.5, Rule 3);
- asks for consent for its own purposes (s.6(1));
- lets people withdraw consent as easily as they gave it (s.6(4));
- keeps proof that notice and consent were given (s.6(10)).

The business remains the **Data Fiduciary**. The software vendor, if it hosts the records, acts as the business's **Data Processor** under a contract (s.8(2)). The tool does not act for the individual, does not let one person manage consents across many unrelated businesses, and does not need registration with the Board.

## Side by side

| | Consent Manager | Consent tool for your website |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | s.2(g), s.6(7)–(9); Rule 4; First Schedule | s.5, s.6(1), s.6(4), s.6(10) duties of the business |
| Works for | The Data Principal (the individual) | The business (Data Fiduciary) |
| Scope | One platform across many Data Fiduciaries | One business's own website, app or forms |
| Registration | Must register with the Board | None |
| Entry conditions | Indian company, net worth ≥ ₹2 crore, independent certification | None beyond normal processor duties |
| Record keeping | At least 7 years (Part B item 4(c)) | Set by the business; Rule 8(3) one-year floor for logs |
| Role of the vendor | Is itself the regulated entity | Data Processor for hosted data, or just a software licensor for self-hosted |

## Do I need a Consent Manager?

For most small businesses, **no**. Section 6(7) says a Data Principal *may* use a Consent Manager. It is an option for the individual, not a duty for the business. A clinic, coaching centre or online store can take consent directly through its own notice, banner and forms.

You may want to integrate with registered Consent Managers later if your customers start using them, especially in sectors such as banking and finance where account-aggregator style flows already exist. That would be an integration, not a change in your role.

## Why the label matters

Calling a website banner a "Consent Manager" can mislead buyers into thinking it is a registered entity, and can mislead visitors into thinking an independent platform is protecting them. For a vendor, behaving like one (for example, offering visitors a single account to manage consents across many unrelated businesses) without registration would be risky.

## FAQ

**Can a software company become a Consent Manager?**
Only an Indian company that meets the First Schedule conditions, including net worth of at least ₹2 crore and independent certification, and is registered by the Board.

**Is a "consent management platform" (CMP) the same as a Consent Manager?**
Not necessarily. "CMP" is an industry term for banner and consent-records software. Only a Board-registered entity is a Consent Manager under the Act.

**Will a consent tool alone take care of DPDP for my website?**
No tool makes you compliant by itself. A consent tool helps you meet the notice, consent, withdrawal and proof requirements; you are still responsible for your purposes, notices and decisions (s.8(1)).

**Do Consent Managers replace my privacy notice?**
No. Every consent request still needs your notice (s.5(1)). Part B item 3 of the First Schedule expects the Consent Manager to keep a record of the notices that preceded or accompanied consent requests.

**When did Consent Manager rules start?**
Rule 4 came into force one year after the Rules were published, in November 2026 (Rule 1(3)). Duties for most other businesses start on 13 May 2027. See the [DPDP 13 May 2027 countdown](/dpdp/dpdp-deadline-13-may-2027).
