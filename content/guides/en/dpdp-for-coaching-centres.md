# DPDP for coaching centres (children's data)

Coaching centres, tuition classes and test-prep institutes collect a lot of data about students who are under 18: names, school, class, marks, photos, attendance, parents' phone numbers and fee details. Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, a **child is anyone who has not completed 18 years** (s.2(f)), so almost every school-level student is a child in law.

The children's rules are among the strictest in the Act and carry a maximum penalty that "may extend to" ₹200 crore (Schedule, item 3), imposed only after a Board inquiry (s.28) finds the breach significant (s.33). The duties apply from **13 May 2027** (Rule 1(4)).

## The three children's rules

1. **Verifiable parental consent** before processing **any** personal data of a child (s.9(1)).
2. **No processing likely to cause any detrimental effect** on the well-being of a child (s.9(2)).
3. **No tracking or behavioural monitoring of children, and no targeted advertising directed at children** (s.9(3)).

## How to verify the parent (Rule 10)

Rule 10(1) asks you to take "appropriate technical and organisational measures" so that the parent's consent is obtained before you process the child's data, and to do due diligence that the person claiming to be the parent is an identifiable adult, by reference to:

- **(a)** reliable details of identity and age already available with you (for example, a parent who is already a verified customer), or
- **(b)** details of identity and age voluntarily provided by the individual, or through a **virtual token** issued by an authorised entity. The Rule's definition includes details or tokens made available and verified by a Digital Locker service provider, such as DigiLocker.

The Rule's illustrations describe a parent identifying herself through the website, app or "other appropriate means", and the Data Fiduciary checking that the parent is an identifiable adult before creating the child's account.

**Practical points (our reading, not legal advice):**

- Ask the age question first. If the student is under 18, collect the parent's details and consent before the student's.
- Record which verification method you used, when, and which notice version the parent saw.
- Do not store copies of Aadhaar or other ID images unless you truly need them. Collecting more ID data creates more risk.
- An offline admission form signed by the parent at the counter, later digitised, is still evidence. Keep it linked to the digital record.

## The Fourth Schedule exemption for educational institutions

Rule 12 and the Fourth Schedule, Part A, item 3 exempt "an educational institution" from s.9(1) and s.9(3), **but only for a narrow purpose**:

> Processing is restricted to tracking and behavioural monitoring (a) for the educational activities of such institution; or (b) in the interests of safety of children enrolled with such institution.

The Schedule defines "educational institution" as "an institution of learning that imparts education, including vocational education".

What this does and does not cover (our reading):

- It covers **tracking and behavioural monitoring** for educational activities or safety, for example attendance tracking, test performance analytics, or CCTV for safety.
- It does **not** remove the parental-consent duty for other processing, such as marketing, sharing data with partners, or publishing toppers' photos for promotion.
- It does **not** allow targeted advertising to children.
- **Whether a private coaching or tuition centre counts as an "educational institution"** is not settled. Many coaching centres do impart education, but you should ask your lawyer before relying on the exemption.

Part B of the Fourth Schedule also lists purposes that are exempt, including item 6: processing to confirm that a person is **not** a child, limited to what that confirmation needs. So an age question is itself allowed.

## Marketing to parents vs targeting children

Marketing is the area where coaching centres most often slip.

| Activity | Typical treatment (our reading) |
|---|---|
| WhatsApp offer to a parent who opted in | Consent of the parent for marketing; easy opt-out (s.6(4)) |
| Ad retargeting pixel on the "Class 10 batch" page aimed at students | Tracking of children is not allowed (s.9(3)); avoid |
| Instagram ads targeted at 15-17 year-olds | Targeted advertising directed at children is not allowed (s.9(3)) |
| Topper photos and marks on hoardings and social media | Needs parental consent for that specific purpose |
| Sharing leads with a partner institute | Needs consent for that purpose; the partner may be a separate Data Fiduciary |
| Attendance SMS to parents | Part of the service the parent signed up for; check basis with your lawyer |

Promotional SMS also needs to follow TRAI's TCCCPR rules.

## Students who turn 18

When a student turns 18, they become an adult Data Principal. Consider asking for their own consent for ongoing purposes like alumni marketing.

## Rights requests from parents and students

Parents (for a child) can ask for a summary of data (s.11), correction or erasure (s.12), grievance redressal (s.13) and nomination (s.14). Publish how to make a request and which identifier you need, such as the enrolment ID or registered mobile (Rule 14(1), 14(5)). Rule 14(5) explicitly lists "enrolment ID" as an identifier. Publish a response period not exceeding ninety days (Rule 14(3)), and put your contact person in every reply (Rule 9).

## Coaching centre checklist

- [ ] Age question at the start of every enquiry and admission form.
- [ ] Parent consent flow with verification method recorded (s.9(1), Rule 10).
- [ ] Separate, unticked consent for marketing, photos and testimonials.
- [ ] No ad pixels, retargeting or behavioural tracking on pages aimed at students (s.9(3)).
- [ ] Notice in English and Hindi or your local language, itemised per purpose (Rule 3, s.5(3)).
- [ ] Contracts with your fee app, LMS, WhatsApp provider and SMS gateway (s.8(2)).
- [ ] Staff access limited to what each role needs; logs kept for one year (Rule 6(1)).
- [ ] Rights request process and grievance contact published.
- [ ] Breach plan (Rule 7). See [breach intimation explained](/dpdp/dpdp-breach-intimation-72-hours-6-hours).
- [ ] Ask your lawyer whether you are an "educational institution" for the Fourth Schedule.

## FAQ

**Is a tick-box saying "I am the parent" enough?**
Rule 10 asks for due diligence that the parent is an identifiable adult, using reliable details you hold, details voluntarily provided, or a virtual token. A bare tick-box with nothing else is weak evidence. Ask your lawyer what is proportionate for your centre.

**Can I use Google Analytics on my website?**
Analytics is a form of tracking. On pages aimed at children it is risky under s.9(3). Many centres turn analytics off by default and enable it only with consent from adult visitors.

**Do the rules apply to offline batches with paper forms?**
Once the data is digitised, yes (s.3(a)(ii)).

**Our students are all 18+ (competitive exam prep). Do the children's rules apply?**
Not to adult students. You still need notices, consent and the other general duties. Ask the age question anyway, since Fourth Schedule Part B item 6 allows processing to confirm someone is not a child.

**Do we need a Data Protection Officer?**
No, unless notified as a Significant Data Fiduciary (s.10). You need a named contact (Rule 9).
