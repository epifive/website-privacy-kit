# DPDP checklist for small business (2027)

If your business collects names, phone numbers, emails, addresses or any other details about people through a website, app, form or WhatsApp, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (the "DPDP Act") applies to you. The DPDP Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), dated 13 November 2025) say how. Most duties that matter to a small business start on **13 May 2027** (Rule 1(4), eighteen months after publication).

This checklist turns the Act and Rules into tasks a clinic, coaching centre, online store, agency or local service business can actually finish. Each item cites the section or rule it comes from, so you or your lawyer can check the source.

## First: does the DPDP Act apply to you?

The Act applies to **digital personal data**, meaning data collected in digital form, or collected on paper and digitised later (s.3(a)). A paper register you later type into Excel or a CRM is covered.

It does **not** apply to data you process for purely personal or domestic purposes, or to data the person has made public themselves (s.3(c)).

Under the Act, your business is a **Data Fiduciary**: you decide why and how the data is used. The person the data is about is the **Data Principal**. Any vendor that handles data for you (hosting, SMS gateway, payment gateway, CRM) is a **Data Processor**.

| What | When | Source |

Some readers count the 18 months from 14 November 2025, which gives 14 May 2027. Plan for 13 May. A proposal to shorten the period to 12 months was reported in January 2026 but had not been notified as of November 2026, so check the e-Gazette before relying on any date. ## The checklist

### 1. Know what data you collect

- [ ] List every place you collect personal data: website forms, checkout, appointment booking, WhatsApp, walk-in registers, job applications.
- [ ] For each, write the **data items** (name, mobile, email, address, date of birth, health details and so on) and the **purpose** (billing, appointment reminders, marketing).
- [ ] Remove fields you do not need. Consent must be "limited to such personal data as is necessary" for the purpose (s.6(1)).

### 2. Write a proper notice

- [ ] Give a notice **before or along with** every consent request (s.5(1)).
- [ ] Make it understandable on its own, without reading other documents (Rule 3(a)).
- [ ] Include an **itemised** list of data and the specific purpose for each (Rule 3(b)(i)-(ii)).
- [ ] Explain how to withdraw consent, how to exercise rights, and how to complain to the Data Protection Board of India, with a link to your website or app (Rule 3(c)).
- [ ] Offer the notice in English or a language from the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution (s.5(3)). Many small businesses offer English and Hindi first.

See [DPDP privacy notice format in Hindi](/dpdp/dpdp-privacy-notice-format-hindi) for a sample structure.

### 3. Ask for consent properly

- [ ] Consent must be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action" (s.6(1)). Unticked boxes, separate choices per purpose.
- [ ] No "by continuing you agree" wording.
- [ ] Give the contact of the person who answers privacy questions in the consent request (s.6(3)).
- [ ] Some processing does not need consent, for example when a customer voluntarily gives data for a specific purpose and has not objected (s.7(a)). Decide this purpose by purpose with your lawyer.
- [ ] Keep a record of every consent: what notice was shown, when, in which language. If questioned, **you** must prove notice and consent were given (s.6(10)).

The Act has no cookie-specific rule. Our reading (not legal advice): cookies and similar identifiers that are personal data need consent unless a legitimate use applies. See [DPDP consent banner](/dpdp/dpdp-consent-banner).

### 4. Make withdrawal easy

- [ ] Withdrawing must be about as easy as giving consent (s.6(4)). If consent took one click, withdrawal should not need an email and a phone call.
- [ ] When someone withdraws, stop processing within a reasonable time and make your vendors stop too (s.6(6)).

### 5. Old customers (existing data)

- [ ] For consent collected before the Act starts, send a notice "as soon as it is reasonably practicable" (s.5(2)). Email, SMS or in-app message all work. Promotional SMS also has to follow TRAI's TCCCPR rules.

### 6. Rights requests

- [ ] Publish how people can make a request and which identifier you need (customer ID, mobile, email) (Rule 14(1), 14(5)).
- [ ] Handle access (s.11), correction and erasure (s.12), grievances (s.13) and nomination (s.14).
- [ ] Publish your response period, which must not exceed **ninety days** (Rule 14(3)). The PIB explainer states 90 days for access, correction, updating and erasure.
- [ ] Publish the business contact of the person who answers privacy questions, and include it in **every** reply (s.8(9), Rule 9).

See [data principal request reply templates](/dpdp/data-principal-request-reply-templates).

### 7. Vendors and contracts

- [ ] List every vendor that receives personal data.
- [ ] Use them "only under a valid contract" (s.8(2)), with a clause on security safeguards (Rule 6(1)(f)).
- [ ] You stay responsible for what they do on your behalf (s.8(1)).

### 8. Security basics

Rule 6(1) lists the minimum: encryption or masking, access control, logs and monitoring, backups, keeping logs for one year (Rule 6(1)(e)), security clauses in vendor contracts, and organisational measures. For a small business this often means strong passwords with two-factor login, staff accounts instead of shared logins, encrypted backups and a reviewed access list.

### 9. Breach plan

- [ ] Tell each affected person **without delay**: what happened, likely consequences, what you are doing, what they can do, and a contact (Rule 7(1)).
- [ ] Tell the Board without delay, then send a detailed report **within 72 hours** of becoming aware (Rule 7(2)).
- [ ] Separately, CERT-In expects certain cyber incidents to be reported within **6 hours** of noticing them (CERT-In Directions, 28 Apr 2022).

See [breach intimation: 72 hours and 6 hours explained](/dpdp/dpdp-breach-intimation-72-hours-6-hours).

### 10. Retention and erasure

- [ ] Erase data when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is over, unless a law needs you to keep it, and make your vendors erase it too (s.8(7)).
- [ ] Keep personal data, traffic data and processing logs for **at least one year** from the processing (Rule 8(3)).
- [ ] Write down a retention period per data type, with the law that requires it (for example tax records).

Fixed erasure periods and the 48-hour pre-erasure notice in Rule 8(1)-(2) apply only to very large e-commerce, gaming and social media platforms listed in the Third Schedule. They are not mandatory for an ordinary small business.

### 11. Children

- [ ] A child is anyone **under 18** (s.2(f)).
- [ ] Get verifiable parental consent before processing a child's data (s.9(1), Rule 10).
- [ ] No tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children (s.9(3)).

Limited exemptions exist for some healthcare and educational bodies (Rule 12, Fourth Schedule). See [DPDP for coaching centres](/dpdp/dpdp-for-coaching-centres).

## What small businesses usually do not need

- **A Data Protection Officer:** only for notified Significant Data Fiduciaries (s.10). You still need a contact person (Rule 9).
- **Data localisation:** transfer abroad is allowed subject to government restrictions (s.16, Rule 15).
- **A Consent Manager:** that is a registered intermediary under s.6(7)-(9). You do not need one. See [Consent Manager vs consent tool](/dpdp/consent-manager-vs-consent-tool).

## Penalties, stated plainly

The Schedule to the Act sets maximum penalties: up to ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards, up to ₹200 crore for failing to give breach intimation or for breaching children's obligations, and up to ₹50 crore for other breaches. They are imposed only after an inquiry by the Board (s.28) finds the breach significant (s.33), and the Board considers factors such as the nature of the breach and steps taken to mitigate it. As of mid-November 2026, news reports said Board members had not yet been appointed.

## FAQ

**Is there a turnover limit below which DPDP does not apply?**
No. The Act has no size threshold. The government may exempt notified classes "including startups" from some provisions (s.17(3)), but we found no such notification as of November 2026.

**Do I need consent for every customer interaction?**
No. Some uses rely on s.7 legitimate uses, such as data a customer voluntarily gives for a specific purpose. Marketing and tracking usually need consent. Decide purpose by purpose.

**Is a privacy policy page enough?**
Usually not. Rule 3 wants an itemised notice shown with the consent request, plus clear ways to withdraw, use rights and complain.

**How long can I take to reply to a request?**
A period you publish, not exceeding ninety days (Rule 14(3)). Shorter is better; 30 days is a common choice.

**Does the 13 May 2027 date apply to everything?**
Most SMB duties, yes. Board machinery and Consent Manager rules started earlier.
